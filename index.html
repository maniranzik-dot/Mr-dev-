📖 Unit 6: Work, Power and Energy

1. Meaning of Work

In Physics, work is done when a force causes an object to move through a distance in the direction of the force.

Conditions for Work to be Done

  • A force must act on an object.
  • The object must move through a distance.
  • The movement must be in the direction of the force.

2. Formula for Work

Work done is calculated by:

Work = Force × Distance

W = F × d

Where:

  • W = Work done (Joules, J)
  • F = Force (Newton, N)
  • d = Distance moved (metres, m)

3. Unit of Work

The SI unit of work is the Joule (J).

One joule is the work done when a force of one Newton moves an object through one metre.

4. Energy

Energy is the ability to do work.

Types of Energy

  • Kinetic energy: Energy possessed by a moving object.
  • Potential energy: Stored energy due to position or condition.
  • Heat energy: Energy related to temperature.
  • Electrical energy: Energy produced by electric charges.
  • Chemical energy: Energy stored in chemical substances.

5. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion.

Formula: KE = ½mv² Where:
  • m = mass (kg)
  • v = velocity (m/s)

6. Potential Energy

Potential energy is stored energy due to the position of an object.

Formula: PE = mgh Where:
  • m = mass (kg)
  • g = gravitational acceleration (m/s²)
  • h = height (m)

7. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done.

Formula: Power = Work ÷ Time

P = W ÷ t

Where:
  • P = Power (Watts, W)
  • W = Work done (J)
  • t = Time (seconds)

8. Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another.

Example:

In a hydroelectric power station:

Potential energy of water → Kinetic energy → Electrical energy

💡 Examples

1. A force of 10 N moves an object 5 m.

Work = 10 × 5 = 50 J

2. A machine does 200 J of work in 10 seconds.

Power = 200 ÷ 10 = 20 W
