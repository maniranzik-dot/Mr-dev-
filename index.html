Unit 10: Skeletal System of Organisms

Meaning of Skeleton

A skeleton is a framework of bones that gives support, protection and shape to an organism.

Functions of Skeleton

  • Provides body support.
  • Protects internal organs.
  • Allows movement with muscles.
  • Maintains body shape.
  • Produces blood cells in bone marrow.

Types of Skeleton

  • Endoskeleton: Skeleton found inside the body (example: humans).
  • Exoskeleton: Hard covering outside the body (example: insects).
  • Hydrostatic skeleton: Support provided by fluid pressure (example: earthworms).

Human Skeleton

  • Skull - protects the brain.
  • Vertebral column - protects spinal cord.
  • Rib cage - protects heart and lungs.
  • Limbs - help movement.

Joints

A joint is a place where two or more bones meet.

  • Fixed joint - no movement.
  • Hinge joint - allows bending and straightening.
  • Ball and socket joint - allows movement in many directions.

Unit 11: Classification of Diseases

Meaning of Disease

A disease is a condition that affects the normal functioning of an organism.

Types of Diseases

  • Communicable diseases: Diseases that spread from one person to another.
    Examples: Malaria, Tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS.
  • Non-communicable diseases: Diseases that do not spread between people.
    Examples: Diabetes, Cancer, Hypertension.

Causes of Diseases

  • Bacteria
  • Viruses
  • Fungi
  • Parasites
  • Poor nutrition
  • Unhealthy lifestyle

Prevention of Diseases

  • Keeping good hygiene.
  • Eating a balanced diet.
  • Vaccination.
  • Using clean water.
  • Regular exercise.

Unit 12: Human Reproductive System

Meaning of Reproduction

Reproduction is the process by which living organisms produce new individuals of the same species.

Male Reproductive System

  • Testes - produce sperm cells and male hormones.
  • Sperm duct - transports sperm.
  • Penis - transfers sperm.
  • Glands - produce fluids that support sperm.

Female Reproductive System

  • Ovaries - produce eggs and hormones.
  • Oviduct/Fallopian tube - site of fertilisation.
  • Uterus - where the baby develops.
  • Vagina - birth canal.

Fertilisation

Fertilisation is the fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.

Importance of Reproduction

  • Ensures continuation of species.
  • Increases population.
  • Passes genetic information to offspring.
