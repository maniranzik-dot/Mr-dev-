🧪 Unit 3: States and Changes of States of Matter

1. Meaning of Matter

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. All substances around us are made of matter.

2. Particle Theory of Matter

The particle theory explains the arrangement and movement of particles that make up matter.

  • All matter is made up of tiny particles.
  • Particles are always moving.
  • There are spaces between particles.
  • Particles attract each other.
  • Increasing temperature increases particle movement.

3. States of Matter

(a) Solid State

A solid has a fixed shape and fixed volume.

Characteristics:
  • Particles are closely packed.
  • Particles only vibrate in fixed positions.
  • Solids are difficult to compress.

Examples: Stone, iron, wood.

(b) Liquid State

A liquid has a fixed volume but no fixed shape.

Characteristics:
  • Particles are close together.
  • Particles can move around.
  • Liquids flow.
  • They take the shape of their container.

Examples: Water, oil, alcohol.

(c) Gaseous State

A gas has no fixed shape and no fixed volume.

Characteristics:
  • Particles are far apart.
  • Particles move freely and quickly.
  • Gases can be compressed easily.

Examples: Oxygen, carbon dioxide, nitrogen.

4. Changes of State of Matter

A change of state is the physical change from one state of matter to another due to gain or loss of heat energy.

1. Melting

Change of a solid into a liquid by heating.

Example: Ice → Water

2. Freezing

Change of a liquid into a solid by cooling.

Example: Water → Ice

3. Evaporation

Change of a liquid into gas at the surface of the liquid.

4. Boiling

Rapid change of liquid into gas throughout the liquid at a fixed temperature.

5. Condensation

Change of gas into liquid by cooling.

6. Sublimation

Direct change of a solid into gas without becoming liquid.

Example: Dry ice.

5. Effect of Temperature on Particles

  • When temperature increases, particles gain energy and move faster.
  • When temperature decreases, particles lose energy and move slower.

6. Diffusion

Diffusion is the movement of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration.

Diffusion happens faster in gases than liquids because gas particles move more freely.
