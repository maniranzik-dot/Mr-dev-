Unit 3: States and Changes of States of Matter

1. Meaning of Matter

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. All substances around us are made up of matter.

2. Particle Theory of Matter

The particle theory explains the arrangement and movement of particles that make up matter.

  • All matter is made up of tiny particles.
  • Particles are always moving.
  • There are spaces between particles.
  • Particles attract each other.
  • Increasing temperature increases particle movement.

3. States of Matter

(a) Solid State

A solid has a fixed shape and fixed volume.

  • Particles are closely packed.
  • Particles vibrate in fixed positions.
  • Solids are difficult to compress.

Examples: Stone, iron, wood.

(b) Liquid State

A liquid has a fixed volume but no fixed shape.

  • Particles are close but can move around.
  • Liquids can flow.
  • They take the shape of their container.

Examples: Water, oil, alcohol.

(c) Gaseous State

A gas has no fixed shape and no fixed volume.

  • Particles are far apart.
  • Particles move freely and quickly.
  • Gases can be compressed easily.

Examples: Oxygen, carbon dioxide, nitrogen.

4. Changes of State of Matter

  • Melting: Change of solid into liquid by heating.
    Example: Ice → Water

  • Freezing: Change of liquid into solid by cooling.
    Example: Water → Ice

  • Evaporation: Change of liquid into gas at the surface of a liquid.

  • Boiling: Rapid change of liquid into gas throughout the liquid.

  • Condensation: Change of gas into liquid by cooling.

Unit 4: Pure Substances and Mixtures

1. Pure Substance

A pure substance is a substance made up of only one type of particle.

Examples: Oxygen, gold, water.

2. Types of Pure Substances

(a) Elements

An element is a pure substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.

Examples: Hydrogen (H), Oxygen (O), Iron (Fe).

(b) Compounds

A compound is a pure substance formed when two or more elements combine chemically.

Examples: Water (H₂O), Carbon dioxide (CO₂).

3. Mixtures

A mixture is a combination of two or more substances that are not chemically combined.

Types of Mixtures

  • Homogeneous mixture: Mixture with uniform composition.
    Example: Salt solution.

  • Heterogeneous mixture: Mixture with different visible parts.
    Example: Sand and water.

4. Separation Methods of Mixtures

  • Filtration: Used to separate insoluble solid from liquid.
  • Evaporation: Used to obtain dissolved solid from a solution.
  • Distillation: Used to separate liquids with different boiling points.
  • Chromatography: Used to separate coloured substances.
  • Decantation: Used to separate liquid from settled solid.
