📖 Unit 9: Heat and Temperature

1. Meaning of Heat

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter body to a colder body due to a difference in temperature.

2. Meaning of Temperature

Temperature is the measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of an object.

3. Difference Between Heat and Temperature

Heat Temperature
Is a form of energy Measures how hot or cold an object is
Measured in Joules (J) Measured in Kelvin (K) or Celsius (°C)
Depends on amount of substance Does not depend on amount of substance

4. Temperature Measurement

Temperature is measured using a thermometer.

Types of Thermometers

  • Clinical thermometer - measures human body temperature.
  • Laboratory thermometer - measures temperature during experiments.
  • Digital thermometer - uses electronic sensors.

5. Temperature Scales

Celsius scale (°C)

  • Freezing point of water = 0°C
  • Boiling point of water = 100°C

Kelvin scale (K)

Kelvin is the SI unit of temperature.

Formula: K = °C + 273

6. Expansion of Materials

Most materials expand when heated and contract when cooled.

Types of Expansion

  • Linear expansion - increase in length.
  • Area expansion - increase in surface area.
  • Volume expansion - increase in volume.

7. Applications of Thermal Expansion

  • Gaps are left between railway lines to allow expansion.
  • Electric wires are not fixed too tightly to allow expansion.
  • Thermometers work due to expansion of liquids.

8. Methods of Heat Transfer

(a) Conduction

Transfer of heat through a substance without movement of the substance.

Common in solids, especially metals.

(b) Convection

Transfer of heat through movement of fluids (liquids and gases).

(c) Radiation

Transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves without needing a medium.

9. Conductors and Insulators

Conductors

Materials that allow heat to pass through easily.

Examples:
  • Copper
  • Aluminium
  • Iron

Insulators

Materials that do not allow heat to pass through easily.

Examples:
  • Wood
  • Plastic
  • Rubber

10. Applications of Heat Transfer

  • Cooking using metal pots.
  • Using wool clothes to reduce heat loss.
  • Solar heaters using radiation.

💡 Examples

1. A metal spoon becomes hot when placed in hot tea because heat is transferred by conduction.
