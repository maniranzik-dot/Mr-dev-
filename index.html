Unit 1: Interdependence Among Organisms in an Ecosystem

1.1 Meaning of an Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with one another and with the non-living components of their environment.

Biotic factors: Living components such as plants, animals, bacteria and fungi.
Abiotic factors: Non-living components such as light, temperature, water, soil, air and minerals.

1.2 Interdependence

Interdependence means that organisms depend on one another for food, shelter, reproduction, protection and other resources.

1.3 Food Chains

A food chain shows the transfer of food energy from one organism to another.

Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk

1.4 Food Webs

A food web consists of several interconnected food chains in an ecosystem.

1.5 Ecological Relationships

  • Predation
  • Competition
  • Mutualism
  • Parasitism
  • Commensalism
Exercise:

What is meant by interdependence among organisms?

Interdependence is the dependence of organisms on one another for resources and survival.

Unit 2: Population Size

2.1 Population

A population is a group of organisms of the same species living in the same area at a particular time.

2.2 Population Size

Population size is the number of individuals of a particular species in a given area.

2.3 Factors Affecting Population Size

  • Birth rate
  • Death rate
  • Immigration
  • Emigration
  • Availability of food
  • Availability of water
  • Predation
  • Diseases
  • Competition
  • Environmental conditions

2.4 Population Sampling

Scientists estimate population size using sampling methods. Common methods include quadrats and transects.

Quadrat: A frame used to sample organisms, especially plants, in a particular area.
Transect: A line or belt used to study how organisms are distributed across an area.
Exercise:

Name four factors that can affect population size.

Birth rate, death rate, immigration and emigration.

Unit 3: Nutrient Cycles

3.1 Meaning of Nutrient Cycles

Nutrient cycles describe the movement and recycling of chemical elements between living organisms and the environment.

3.2 Carbon Cycle

Carbon moves between the atmosphere, plants, animals, soil and other parts of the environment.

  • Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
  • Respiration releases carbon dioxide.
  • Decomposition releases carbon dioxide.
  • Combustion releases carbon dioxide.

3.3 Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen is essential for making proteins and nucleic acids. Nitrogen is recycled through processes involving bacteria.

  • Nitrogen fixation
  • Nitrification
  • Assimilation
  • Ammonification
  • Denitrification

3.4 Importance of Nutrient Cycles

  • They recycle essential nutrients.
  • They maintain soil fertility.
  • They support plant growth.
  • They maintain ecosystem balance.
Exercise:

Why are nutrient cycles important in an ecosystem?

They recycle essential nutrients so that organisms can continue to obtain the materials needed for growth and survival.

Unit 4: Effects of Human Activities on Ecosystems

4.1 Human Activities

  • Deforestation
  • Agriculture
  • Mining
  • Industrialisation
  • Urbanisation
  • Fishing
  • Hunting
  • Burning of fuels

4.2 Deforestation

Deforestation is the removal of trees from forests. It can lead to soil erosion, loss of habitats and reduced biodiversity.

4.3 Pollution

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances into the environment.

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Soil pollution
  • Noise pollution

4.4 Effects on Ecosystems

  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Destruction of habitats
  • Soil erosion
  • Climate change
  • Water pollution
  • Reduced soil fertility
Exercise:

Give three effects of deforestation.

Habitat destruction, soil erosion and loss of biodiversity.

Unit 5: Effects of Human Activities on Ecosystems — Conservation and Sustainability

5.1 Conservation

Conservation is the protection, management and wise use of natural resources and living organisms.

5.2 Sustainable Use of Resources

Sustainability means using resources in a way that meets present needs without preventing future generations from meeting their needs.

5.3 Methods of Conservation

  • Afforestation and reforestation
  • Creation of national parks
  • Protection of endangered species
  • Controlled hunting and fishing
  • Recycling and reuse of materials
  • Proper waste management
  • Use of renewable energy
  • Environmental education

5.4 Importance of Conservation

  • Protects biodiversity.
  • Protects natural habitats.
  • Prevents extinction.
  • Maintains ecosystem balance.
  • Protects natural resources for future generations.
Exercise:

What is conservation?

Conservation is the protection, management and wise use of natural resources and living organisms.
