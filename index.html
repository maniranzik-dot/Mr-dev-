📖 Unit 2: Qualitative Analysis of Linear Motion

1. Meaning of Motion

Motion is the change in position of an object with time relative to a reference point.

An object is said to be moving if its position changes with time.

2. Linear Motion

Linear motion is the movement of an object along a straight line.

Examples:

  • A car moving on a straight road.
  • A stone falling vertically downwards.
  • A person walking in a straight path.

3. Types of Linear Motion

(a) Uniform Motion

Uniform motion is when an object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time.

Example: A car moving at constant speed on a straight road.

(b) Non-uniform Motion

Non-uniform motion is when an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time.

Example: A bus stopping and starting in a town.

4. Distance and Displacement

Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object.

  • It is a scalar quantity.
  • It has magnitude only.
  • SI unit is metre (m).

Displacement

Displacement is the shortest distance between the starting point and ending point in a given direction.

  • It is a vector quantity.
  • It has magnitude and direction.
  • SI unit is metre (m).

5. Speed

Speed is the distance travelled per unit time.

Formula:

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

SI unit: metres per second (m/s)

6. Velocity

Velocity is the displacement travelled per unit time in a given direction.

Formula:

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

7. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time.

Formula:

Acceleration = Change in velocity ÷ Time

SI unit: metres per second squared (m/s²)

8. Motion Graphs

Distance-Time Graph

  • A straight line shows uniform speed.
  • A curved line shows changing speed.

Velocity-Time Graph

  • Horizontal line shows constant velocity.
  • Sloping line shows acceleration.

9. Effects of Forces on Motion

A force can change the speed, direction or shape of an object.

Examples:

  • Pushing a ball makes it move.
  • Brakes reduce the speed of a bicycle.

💡 Examples

1. A car travels 100 m in 20 seconds.

Speed = 100 ÷ 20 = 5 m/s

2. A runner changes velocity from 5 m/s to 15 m/s in 5 seconds.

Acceleration = (15 - 5) ÷ 5 = 2 m/s²
