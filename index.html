Unit 9: Heat and Temperature

1. Meaning of Heat

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter body to a colder body because of temperature difference.

2. Meaning of Temperature

Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

3. Difference Between Heat and Temperature

  • Heat is a form of energy, while temperature measures how hot or cold an object is.
  • Heat is measured in Joules (J), while temperature is measured in degrees Celsius (°C) or Kelvin (K).

4. Measuring Temperature

Temperature is measured using a thermometer.

Types of Thermometers

  • Clinical thermometer - measures human body temperature.
  • Laboratory thermometer - measures temperature of substances.
  • Digital thermometer - uses electronic sensors.

5. Temperature Scales

  • Celsius scale (°C)
  • Kelvin scale (K)
  • Fahrenheit scale (°F)

6. Methods of Heat Transfer

(a) Conduction

Conduction is the transfer of heat through a substance without movement of the substance itself.

Example: A metal spoon becoming hot in hot water.

(b) Convection

Convection is the transfer of heat through fluids by movement of particles.

Example: Heating water.

(c) Radiation

Radiation is the transfer of heat energy through electromagnetic waves without needing a medium.

Example: Heat from the Sun.

7. Effects of Heat

  • Expansion of substances.
  • Change of state.
  • Increase in temperature.

Unit 10: Magnetism

1. Meaning of Magnetism

Magnetism is the force produced by magnets which attracts or repels magnetic materials.

2. Magnetic Materials

  • Iron
  • Steel
  • Nickel
  • Cobalt

3. Properties of Magnets

  • Magnets have two poles: North and South.
  • Like poles repel each other.
  • Unlike poles attract each other.
  • Magnets attract magnetic materials.

4. Magnetic Field

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet where magnetic force can be experienced.

5. Types of Magnets

  • Permanent magnets - retain magnetism for a long time.
  • Temporary magnets - lose magnetism easily.
  • Electromagnets - magnets produced by electric current.

6. Uses of Magnets

  • Electric motors.
  • Speakers.
  • Magnetic locks.
  • Compasses.
  • Lifting magnetic materials.
