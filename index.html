Unit 14: Physical Properties That Affect Plant Growth

14.1 Introduction

Plant growth is affected by several physical conditions in the environment. Important factors include temperature, light, water, humidity, air movement and soil conditions.

14.2 Temperature

Temperature affects the rate of physiological processes in plants. Each plant has a suitable temperature range for healthy growth.

  • Very low temperatures can slow plant processes.
  • Suitable temperatures support normal growth.
  • Very high temperatures can damage plant tissues.

14.3 Light

Light provides the energy needed for photosynthesis. The amount, duration and quality of light can affect plant growth.

  • Light intensity
  • Duration of light
  • Light quality

14.4 Water

Water is essential for photosynthesis, transport of substances and maintaining the firmness of plant cells.

Too little water can cause wilting, while excessive water can reduce oxygen availability around roots.

14.5 Humidity

Humidity is the amount of water vapour present in the air. It affects the rate at which plants lose water through transpiration.

14.6 Air Movement

Wind and air movement can affect transpiration, gas exchange and temperature around plants.

14.7 Soil Physical Properties

The physical properties of soil influence root growth and water and air availability.

  • Soil texture
  • Soil structure
  • Soil porosity
  • Water-holding capacity
  • Soil temperature

14.8 Importance of These Factors

Understanding physical conditions helps farmers and gardeners improve plant growth and manage crops effectively.

Unit 15: Environmental Phenomena and Related Plants

15.1 Environmental Phenomena

Environmental phenomena are natural events or changes in the environment that can influence plants and other living organisms.

15.2 Drought

Drought is a prolonged period of unusually low rainfall or water availability.

Drought can cause water stress, wilting and reduced plant growth.

15.3 Flooding

Flooding occurs when water covers land that is normally dry. Excess water can reduce oxygen available to plant roots.

15.4 Strong Winds

Strong winds can damage leaves, stems and branches. They can also increase water loss through transpiration.

15.5 Temperature Extremes

Extremely high or low temperatures can affect plant growth and may damage plant tissues.

15.6 Effects of Environmental Phenomena on Plants

  • Reduced growth
  • Wilting
  • Leaf damage
  • Root damage
  • Reduced crop yield
  • Plant death in severe conditions

15.7 Plant Adaptation

Plants have different adaptations that help them survive environmental conditions.

Examples

  • Deep roots can help plants access water during dry conditions.
  • Some plants have thick leaves that store water.
  • Small or modified leaves can reduce water loss.
  • Strong stems can help plants withstand wind.

15.8 Importance of Understanding Environmental Phenomena

Understanding environmental phenomena helps people predict risks, protect crops and select suitable farming practices.
