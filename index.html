📖 Unit 13: Rectilinear Propagation of Light

1. Meaning of Light

Light is a form of energy that enables us to see objects.

2. Sources of Light

A source of light is an object that produces its own light.

Types of light sources:

  • Natural sources: Sun, stars, lightning.
  • Artificial sources: Electric bulbs, candles, lamps.

3. Rectilinear Propagation of Light

Rectilinear propagation means that light travels in straight lines when it moves through a uniform transparent medium.

4. Evidence of Rectilinear Propagation

  • Formation of shadows.
  • Eclipses of the Sun and Moon.
  • Pinhole camera.
  • Light rays passing through small holes.

5. Rays and Beams of Light

A light ray is a straight line showing the direction in which light travels.

A beam of light is a group of light rays travelling together.

6. Shadows

A shadow is a dark region formed when an opaque object blocks the path of light.

Parts of a Shadow

  • Umbra: The darkest part where light is completely blocked.
  • Penumbra: The lighter region where light is partially blocked.

7. Conditions for Formation of Shadows

  • A source of light.
  • An opaque object.
  • A screen where the shadow is formed.

8. Pinhole Camera

A pinhole camera is a simple device that forms an image using a small hole through which light passes.

Properties of Image in a Pinhole Camera

  • Real image.
  • Inverted image.
  • Smaller or larger depending on distance.

9. Eclipse

Solar Eclipse

Occurs when the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth, blocking sunlight from reaching part of the Earth.

Lunar Eclipse

Occurs when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, causing the Earth's shadow to fall on the Moon.

10. Applications of Rectilinear Propagation

  • Photography using cameras.
  • Design of optical instruments.
  • Understanding formation of shadows and eclipses.

3. Solar eclipses occur due to the straight-line movement of light.
