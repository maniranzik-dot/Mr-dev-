Unit 4: Magnifying Instruments and Biology Drawings

1. Magnifying Instruments

Magnifying instruments are tools used to enlarge small objects so that they can be seen clearly.

Examples of Magnifying Instruments

  • Hand lens: Used for observing small objects.
  • Light microscope: Used to observe cells and microorganisms.
  • Electron microscope: Used to view very tiny structures.

Parts of a Light Microscope

  • Eyepiece lens
  • Objective lenses
  • Stage
  • Stage clips
  • Mirror or light source
  • Coarse adjustment knob
  • Fine adjustment knob

Biology Drawings

Biological drawings are scientific drawings of organisms or biological structures observed during practical work.

Rules of Biological Drawing

  • Use a sharp pencil.
  • Draw clear single lines.
  • Do not shade.
  • Label correctly.
  • Give a suitable title.

Unit 5: Plant and Animal Cells

Meaning of Cell

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.

Plant Cell Structures

  • Cell wall
  • Cell membrane
  • Cytoplasm
  • Nucleus
  • Chloroplasts
  • Large vacuole

Animal Cell Structures

  • Cell membrane
  • Cytoplasm
  • Nucleus
  • Small vacuoles

Differences Between Plant and Animal Cells

  • Plant cells have a cell wall; animal cells do not.
  • Plant cells have chloroplasts; animal cells do not.
  • Plant cells have a large vacuole; animal cells have small vacuoles.

Functions of Cell Parts

  • Nucleus - controls cell activities.
  • Cytoplasm - site of chemical reactions.
  • Cell membrane - controls movement of substances.
  • Chloroplast - contains chlorophyll for photosynthesis.

Unit 6: Levels of Organisation in Multicellular Organisms

Multicellular organisms are made up of many cells working together.

Levels of Organisation

  • Cell: Basic unit of life.
  • Tissue: Group of similar cells performing the same function.
  • Organ: Group of tissues working together.
  • Organ system: Group of organs working together.
  • Organism: A complete living thing.

Examples

  • Muscle cell → Muscle tissue → Heart → Circulatory system → Human
  • Palisade cell → Leaf tissue → Leaf → Shoot system → Plant

Importance of Organisation

  • Allows division of labour.
  • Makes organisms work efficiently.
  • Helps organisms survive.
