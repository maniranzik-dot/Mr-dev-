Unit 1: Chemical Bonding

1. Meaning of Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonding is the force of attraction that holds atoms or ions together to form molecules and compounds.

2. Why Atoms Form Bonds

  • Atoms form bonds to become more stable.
  • They tend to achieve a stable outer electron arrangement.
  • Bond formation can lower the energy of the atoms.

3. Types of Chemical Bonds

Ionic Bond

An ionic bond is formed when electrons are transferred from one atom to another, usually from a metal to a non-metal.

Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share electrons.

Metallic bonding is the attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons.

Unit 2: Trends in Properties of Elements in the Periodic Table

Periodic Table

The periodic table arranges elements according to increasing atomic number and recurring chemical properties.

Groups and Periods

  • A group is a vertical column.
  • A period is a horizontal row.

Important Trends

Property Across a Period Down a Group
Atomic radius Generally decreases Generally increases
Ionisation energy Generally increases Generally decreases
Metallic character Decreases Increases

Group 1

Group 1 contains alkali metals such as lithium, sodium and potassium. They are generally very reactive.

Li → Na → K → Rb → Cs

Group 17

Group 17 contains halogens such as fluorine, chlorine and bromine. They are reactive non-metals.

Unit 5: Categories of Chemical Reactions

Meaning

A chemical reaction is a process in which reactants are changed into new substances called products.

1. Combination Reaction

Two or more substances combine to form one product.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

2. Decomposition Reaction

One compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

3. Displacement Reaction

A more reactive element replaces a less reactive element in a compound.

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

4. Neutralisation

An acid reacts with a base to form salt and water.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Signs of Chemical Reaction

  • Colour change.
  • Gas production.
  • Formation of a precipitate.
  • Temperature change.
  • Light production.

Unit 7: Mole Concept

Meaning of Mole

A mole is the amount of substance containing approximately 6.02 × 10²³ particles.

Avogadro constant = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

Number of Moles

n = m / M

Where:
n = number of moles
m = mass in grams
M = molar mass in g/mol

Example

Find the number of moles in 18 g of H₂O.

Molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol

n = 18 / 18

n = 1 mol

Particles

Number of particles = n × 6.02 × 10²³

Unit 9: Electrolysis and Non-electrolysis

Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic substance using electrical energy.

Main Components

  • Electrolyte.
  • Anode.
  • Cathode.
  • Power source.
  • Connecting wires.
🔋 Battery

+ → 🟥 Anode | Electrolyte | Cathode 🟦 ← −

Anions → Anode
Cations → Cathode

Electrolysis of Water

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

Hydrogen is produced at the cathode and oxygen is produced at the anode under appropriate electrolysis conditions.

Electrolytes

Electrolytes are substances that conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in water because they contain mobile ions.

Non-electrolytes

Non-electrolytes do not conduct electricity because they do not provide mobile ions in solution.

Applications

  • Electroplating.
  • Extraction of metals.
  • Purification of metals.
  • Manufacture of chemicals.

Unit 10: Organic Chemistry

Meaning

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry concerned mainly with carbon compounds.

Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

Main Hydrocarbon Families

Family Bond Example
Alkanes Single Methane CH₄
Alkenes Double Ethene C₂H₄
Alkynes Triple Ethyne C₂H₂

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons containing only single carbon-carbon bonds.

General formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Examples:
CH₄ = Methane
C₂H₆ = Ethane
C₃H₈ = Propane

Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond.

General formula: CₙH₂ₙ
C₂H₄ = Ethene

Alkynes

Alkynes contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond.

General formula: CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
C₂H₂ = Ethyne

Uses of Organic Compounds

  • Fuels.
  • Plastics.
  • Medicines.
  • Solvents.
  • Manufacturing materials.
