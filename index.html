📖 Unit 3: Force

1. Meaning of Force

Force is a push or pull that acts on an object and can change its state of motion or shape.

The SI unit of force is Newton (N).

2. Effects of Force

A force can produce the following effects:

  • Change the speed of an object.
  • Change the direction of motion.
  • Change the shape or size of an object.
  • Stop a moving object.

3. Types of Forces

(a) Contact Forces

These are forces that act when objects are touching each other.

Examples:
  • Frictional force
  • Muscular force
  • Tension force

(b) Non-contact Forces

These are forces that act without physical contact.

Examples:
  • Gravitational force
  • Magnetic force
  • Electrostatic force

4. Friction

Friction is a force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact.

Advantages of friction:

  • Allows us to walk without slipping.
  • Helps vehicles to stop using brakes.
  • Allows writing on paper.

Disadvantages of friction:

  • Causes wear and tear of machines.
  • Produces unwanted heat.
  • Reduces efficiency of machines.

5. Gravity

Gravity is the force that attracts objects towards the centre of the Earth.

The force of gravity on an object is called weight.

Formula: Weight (W) = Mass (m) × Gravitational acceleration (g)

W = mg

6. Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

Balanced Forces

Forces acting on an object are equal and opposite, causing no change in motion.

Unbalanced Forces

Forces are not equal, causing a change in motion.

7. Measuring Force

Force is measured using an instrument called a spring balance or Newton meter.

8. Force and Motion

A force can accelerate, slow down or change the direction of a moving object.

💡 Examples

1. Pushing a door applies force and changes its position.

2. A magnet attracting iron is an example of non-contact force.

3. Friction between tyres and road helps a car move safely.
