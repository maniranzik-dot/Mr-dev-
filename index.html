📖 Unit 3: Cells

Meaning of a cell

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of a living organism. All living organisms are made up of cells.

Cell theory

  • All living organisms are made of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of life.
  • New cells come from existing cells.

Types of cells

1. Plant cell

Plant cells contain structures such as:

  • Cell wall
  • Chloroplast
  • Large vacuole
  • Cell membrane
  • Nucleus
  • Cytoplasm

2. Animal cell

  • Cell membrane
  • Nucleus
  • Cytoplasm
  • Mitochondria

Functions of cell parts

  • Nucleus: Controls activities of the cell.
  • Cell membrane: Controls movement of substances in and out.
  • Cytoplasm: Site of chemical reactions.
  • Mitochondria: Releases energy through respiration.
  • Chloroplast: Contains chlorophyll for photosynthesis.
  • Cell wall: Provides support and protection.

Microscope

A microscope is an instrument used to observe small objects that cannot be seen with the naked eye.

💡 Example

A plant cell has a chloroplast because plants make food by photosynthesis.

📖 Unit 4: Biological Organisation

Levels of organisation

Living organisms are organised from simple structures to complex structures.

  • Cell
  • Tissue
  • Organ
  • Organ system
  • Organism

Cells

Cells are the smallest units capable of carrying out life processes.

Tissues

A tissue is a group of similar cells working together to perform a specific function.

Organs

An organ is made up of different tissues working together.

Organ systems

An organ system is a group of organs working together to perform a major function.

Examples

  • Digestive system - digestion of food.
  • Respiratory system - exchange of gases.
  • Circulatory system - transport of substances.
