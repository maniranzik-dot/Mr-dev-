📖 Unit 4: Newton's Laws of Motion

1. Introduction

Newton's laws of motion explain the relationship between force, mass and motion of objects. They were developed by Sir Isaac Newton.

2. Newton's First Law of Motion

Newton's first law states that:

An object remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.

Explanation:

  • A stationary object remains stationary until a force acts on it.
  • A moving object continues moving unless a force stops or changes it.

Examples:

  • A book on a table remains at rest until someone pushes it.
  • Passengers move forward when a moving bus stops suddenly.

3. Inertia

Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist any change in its state of motion.

Types of inertia:

  • Inertia of rest: Objects resist being moved.
  • Inertia of motion: Moving objects resist stopping.
  • Inertia of direction: Objects resist change of direction.

4. Newton's Second Law of Motion

Newton's second law states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the force applied and inversely proportional to its mass.

Formula: F = ma Where:
  • F = Force (Newton, N)
  • m = Mass (kilogram, kg)
  • a = Acceleration (m/s²)

Meaning:

  • Increasing force increases acceleration.
  • Increasing mass reduces acceleration.

Example:

A force of 20 N acts on a mass of 5 kg.

a = F ÷ m = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s²

5. Newton's Third Law of Motion

Newton's third law states that:

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Examples:

  • A rocket moves upward because gases are pushed downward.
  • Walking is possible because the foot pushes the ground backward and the ground pushes the body forward.
  • A gun moves backward when a bullet is fired.

6. Momentum

Momentum is the quantity of motion possessed by a moving object.

Formula: Momentum = Mass × Velocity

p = mv

Where:
  • p = momentum
  • m = mass
  • v = velocity

7. Applications of Newton's Laws

  • Designing vehicles and safety belts.
  • Understanding movement of rockets.
  • Sports activities such as kicking and throwing balls.
