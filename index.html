📖 Unit 11: Electrostatics

1. Meaning of Electrostatics

Electrostatics is the study of electric charges at rest (not moving).

2. Electric Charge

Electric charge is a property of matter that causes attraction or repulsion between objects.

3. Types of Electric Charges

  • Positive charge (+)
  • Negative charge (-)

4. Law of Electric Charges

  • Like charges repel each other.
  • Unlike charges attract each other.

5. Structure of an Atom

An atom contains:

  • Protons - positively charged particles.
  • Electrons - negatively charged particles.
  • Neutrons - particles with no charge.

6. Charging of Objects

Charging is the process of giving an object an electric charge.

Methods of Charging

(a) Charging by Friction

Objects become charged when they are rubbed together and electrons move from one object to another.

(b) Charging by Contact

A charged object transfers charge when it touches another object.

(c) Charging by Induction

An object becomes charged without direct contact with a charged object.

7. Conductors and Insulators

Conductors

Materials that allow electric charges to move easily.

Examples:
  • Metals
  • Copper
  • Aluminium

Insulators

Materials that do not allow charges to move freely.

Examples:
  • Plastic
  • Rubber
  • Glass

8. Electric Field

An electric field is the region around a charged object where another charge experiences an electric force.

9. Electrostatic Effects in Daily Life

  • Lightning during storms.
  • Photocopiers and printers.
  • Static electricity when combing hair.
  • Spray painting.

10. Dangers of Electrostatic Charges

  • Lightning can cause damage and injuries.
  • Static sparks can cause fires near fuel.

11. Lightning Protection

A lightning conductor is a metal rod placed on buildings to safely transfer electric charges to the ground.
