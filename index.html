📖 Unit 1: Laboratory Safety Rules and Measurements of Physics Quantities

1. Physics Laboratory

A physics laboratory is a special room where experiments and practical activities are carried out to study physical phenomena.

2. Laboratory Safety Rules

Safety rules are instructions that help to prevent accidents and injuries during experiments.

Important safety rules:

  • Always follow instructions given by the teacher.
  • Wear protective equipment such as goggles and gloves when necessary.
  • Do not play or run inside the laboratory.
  • Do not touch electrical equipment with wet hands.
  • Handle glass apparatus carefully.
  • Report accidents immediately to the teacher.
  • Keep the laboratory clean and organized.
  • Do not taste or smell unknown chemicals directly.

3. Laboratory Apparatus

Laboratory apparatus are tools used during experiments.

Examples:

  • Metre rule - used to measure length.
  • Stopwatch - used to measure time.
  • Thermometer - used to measure temperature.
  • Balance - used to measure mass.
  • Measuring cylinder - used to measure liquid volume.

4. Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is a quantity that can be measured and expressed using a number and a unit.

5. SI Units

The International System of Units (SI) is the standard system used for measurements worldwide.

Physical quantity SI unit Symbol
Length Metre m
Mass Kilogram kg
Time Second s
Temperature Kelvin K
Electric current Ampere A

6. Measuring Length

Length is the distance between two points.

Common instruments used:

  • Metre rule
  • Vernier calipers
  • Micrometer screw gauge

7. Measurement Errors

An error is the difference between a measured value and the true value.

Types of errors:

  • Parallax error: caused by reading a scale from the wrong angle.
  • Zero error: when an instrument does not start from zero correctly.
  • Human error: mistakes made by the person measuring.

💡 Examples

1. Measuring the length of a book using a ruler.

2. Measuring temperature of water using a thermometer.

3. Measuring time taken by a runner using a stopwatch.

📖 Unit 13: Genetics and Heredity

Meaning of genetics

Genetics is the study of inheritance and variation in living organisms.

Heredity

Heredity is the passing of characteristics from parents to offspring.

Genes and chromosomes

  • Genes are units of inheritance that control characteristics.
  • Genes are found on chromosomes inside the nucleus of cells.
  • Chromosomes are structures that carry genetic information.

Variation

Variation refers to differences between individuals of the same species.

Types of variation

  • Inherited variation: Passed from parents through genes.
  • Environmental variation: Caused by environmental factors.

Examples of inherited characteristics

  • Eye colour
  • Blood group
  • Natural hair type

Examples of environmental characteristics

  • Body weight affected by diet.
  • Skin colour affected by sunlight exposure.
`, example:`

💡 Example

Children may look like their parents because they inherit genes from both parents.

📖 Unit 14: Ecology and Environment

Meaning of ecology

Ecology is the study of relationships between living organisms and their environment.

Environment

The environment consists of all factors that affect living organisms.

Components of environment

Biotic factors

These are living components.

  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Microorganisms

Abiotic factors

These are non-living components.

  • Water
  • Temperature
  • Light
  • Soil
  • Air

Food chains

A food chain shows the transfer of energy from one organism to another.

Producers and consumers

  • Producers make their own food, for example green plants.
  • Consumers obtain food by eating other organisms.
  • Decomposers break down dead organisms.

Conservation of environment

  • Planting trees.
  • Reducing pollution.
  • Protecting wildlife.
`, example:`

💡 Example

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake

This is an example of a food chain.
