Unit 3: Force

1. Meaning of Force

Force is a push or pull that can change the state of rest or motion of an object.

2. Effects of Force

  • It can make a stationary object move.
  • It can stop a moving object.
  • It can change the speed of an object.
  • It can change the direction of motion.
  • It can change the shape of an object.

3. Types of Forces

  • Contact forces: Forces that act when objects touch.
  • Non-contact forces: Forces that act without touching.

Examples of Forces

  • Gravitational force
  • Frictional force
  • Magnetic force
  • Electrical force
  • Muscular force

4. Measuring Force

Force is measured using a spring balance. The SI unit of force is Newton (N).

5. Mass and Weight

Mass is the amount of matter in an object. It is measured in kilograms (kg).

Weight is the force with which gravity attracts an object.

Formula: Weight = Mass × Gravitational acceleration

Unit 4: Newton's Laws of Motion

1. Newton's First Law

An object remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity unless acted upon by an external force.

Examples

  • A book remains on a table until pushed.
  • A moving ball stops because of friction.

2. Newton's Second Law

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the force applied and inversely proportional to its mass.

Formula: F = m × a

  • F = Force (Newton)
  • m = Mass (kg)
  • a = Acceleration (m/s²)

3. Newton's Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Examples

  • A rocket moves upward because gases are pushed downward.
  • Walking happens because feet push the ground backward.
