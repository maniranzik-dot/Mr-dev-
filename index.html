Unit 16: Social Factors That Affect Good Health

16.1 Meaning of Good Health

Good health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being. It is influenced by many factors in a person's environment and society.

16.2 Social Factors Affecting Health

  • Education
  • Income and poverty
  • Housing
  • Nutrition
  • Sanitation
  • Access to healthcare
  • Culture and beliefs
  • Family and community support

16.3 Effects of Poor Living Conditions

  • Increased risk of disease
  • Poor nutrition
  • Unsafe drinking water
  • Poor sanitation
  • Increased stress

16.4 Improving Good Health

  • Eating a balanced diet
  • Maintaining good hygiene
  • Using clean water
  • Seeking medical care when necessary
  • Getting health education
  • Keeping the environment clean

Unit 18: HIV and AIDS — Stigma, Treatment, Care and Support

18.1 Meaning of HIV

HIV stands for Human Immunodeficiency Virus. It attacks cells of the immune system and can weaken the body's ability to fight infections.

18.2 Meaning of AIDS

AIDS stands for Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome. It describes advanced HIV infection with severe damage to the immune system.

18.3 HIV Transmission

  • Unprotected sexual contact
  • Sharing contaminated needles or other injecting equipment
  • Transmission from mother to child during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding
  • Transfusion of contaminated blood where blood is not properly screened

18.4 HIV Prevention

  • Testing and knowing one's HIV status
  • Using appropriate prevention methods
  • Avoiding sharing needles
  • Using screened blood
  • Accessing prevention and treatment services

18.5 HIV Treatment

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) helps control HIV. It reduces the amount of virus in the body and helps protect the immune system.

18.6 Stigma and Discrimination

People living with HIV should be treated with respect. Stigma and discrimination can prevent people from seeking testing, treatment and support.

18.7 Care and Support

  • Medical care
  • Emotional support
  • Family support
  • Healthy nutrition
  • Adherence to prescribed treatment

Unit 20: Genetics

20.1 Meaning of Genetics

Genetics is the study of heredity and variation in organisms.

20.2 Genes

A gene is a section of DNA that contains information for a particular characteristic or biological function.

20.3 Chromosomes

Chromosomes are structures made mainly of DNA and proteins. Genes are located on chromosomes.

20.4 Heredity

Heredity is the transmission of characteristics from parents to their offspring through genetic material.

20.5 Genetic Variation

  • Mutation
  • Meiosis
  • Crossing over
  • Independent assortment
  • Random fertilisation

20.6 Dominant and Recessive Alleles

A dominant allele can determine a phenotype when present in a suitable genotype, while a recessive allele is expressed when no dominant allele masks its effect.

Unit 22: Variation and Adaptive Features

22.1 Meaning of Variation

Variation refers to differences in characteristics between individuals of the same species.

22.2 Causes of Variation

  • Genetic differences
  • Mutation
  • Recombination
  • Environmental factors

22.3 Types of Variation

Continuous variation: Characteristics show a range of values, such as height and body mass.
Discontinuous variation: Characteristics fall into distinct categories, often determined strongly by genes.

22.4 Adaptive Features

An adaptive feature is an inherited characteristic that increases an organism's ability to survive and reproduce in its environment.

22.5 Examples of Adaptations

  • Camouflage
  • Thick fur in cold environments
  • Water-conserving features in desert plants
  • Webbed feet in some aquatic birds
Exercise:

Unit 23: Natural and Artificial Selection

23.1 Natural Selection

Natural selection is the process in which individuals with advantageous inherited characteristics are more likely to survive and reproduce.

23.2 Steps in Natural Selection

  1. Individuals in a population show variation.
  2. There is competition for limited resources.
  3. Some individuals have advantageous characteristics.
  4. Those individuals are more likely to survive and reproduce.
  5. Advantageous characteristics become more common over generations.

23.3 Artificial Selection

Artificial selection occurs when humans choose organisms with desirable characteristics and breed them to produce offspring with those characteristics.

23.4 Examples

  • Selective breeding of cattle
  • Selective breeding of chickens
  • Improvement of crop varieties
  • Breeding dogs for desired characteristics

23.5 Natural vs Artificial Selection

Natural selection → Environment determines which characteristics are favoured.
Artificial selection → Humans choose which characteristics are favoured.
Exercise:

What is the difference between natural and artificial selection?

Natural selection is driven by environmental conditions, while artificial selection is controlled by humans who select organisms with desirable characteristics for breeding.
