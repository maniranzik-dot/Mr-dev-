Unit 11: Magnetic Field and Electromagnetism

Magnetism

Magnetism is the force of attraction or repulsion produced by magnets.

Magnetic Field

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet where magnetic forces can be experienced.

North Pole (N)
→ → → Magnetic Field Lines → → →
South Pole (S)

Electromagnetism

Electromagnetism is the production of a magnetic field by an electric current.

Factors affecting strength of an electromagnet

  • Increasing current
  • Increasing number of coil turns
  • Using an iron core

Applications

  • Electric bells
  • Relays
  • Electric motors
  • Generators

Unit 12: Application of Electrostatics

Meaning of Electrostatics

Electrostatics is the study of electric charges at rest.

Types of Charges

  • Positive charge
  • Negative charge

Like charges repel each other while unlike charges attract.

+ + = Repulsion
+ - = Attraction

Charging Methods

  • Charging by friction
  • Charging by conduction
  • Charging by induction

Applications

  • Photocopiers
  • Spray painting
  • Lightning protection

Unit 13: Arrangement of Resistors in Electric Circuits

Resistance

Resistance is the opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current.

V = IR

V = Voltage
I = Current
R = Resistance

Series Connection

Resistors are connected one after another.

R(total) = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
Battery
— R₁ — R₂ — R₃ —

Parallel Connection

Resistors are connected on separate branches.

1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
  R₁
—| |—
  R₂
—| |—

Advantages

  • Series: simple circuit arrangement.
  • Parallel: each device works independently.
