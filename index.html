Unit 1: Chemical Bonding

Meaning of Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonding is the force of attraction that holds atoms or ions together to form molecules and compounds.

Why atoms form bonds

  • To become more stable.
  • To achieve a complete outer electron shell.
  • To reduce energy.

Types of Chemical Bonds

1. Ionic Bond

An ionic bond is formed by transfer of electrons from one atom to another.

Example:
Na + Cl → NaCl

2. Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share electrons.

Examples:
H₂, O₂, H₂O

3. Metallic Bond

Metallic bonding is the attraction between positive metal ions and free moving electrons.

Properties of Compounds

  • Ionic compounds have high melting points.
  • Covalent compounds usually have low melting points.
  • Metals conduct electricity.

Unit 2: Trends in Properties of Elements in the Periodic Table

Periodic Table

The periodic table is an arrangement of elements according to their atomic numbers and properties.

Periods and Groups

  • Periods are horizontal rows.
  • Groups are vertical columns.

Trends Across a Period

  • Atomic size decreases.
  • Ionization energy increases.
  • Metallic character decreases.

Trends Down a Group

  • Atomic size increases.
  • Reactivity of metals increases.
  • Number of electron shells increases.

Examples

Group 1 elements:
Li, Na, K
They are reactive metals.

Unit 3: Water Pollution

Meaning of Water Pollution

Water pollution is the contamination of water by harmful substances that make it unsafe.

Causes of Water Pollution

  • Industrial waste.
  • Sewage disposal.
  • Agricultural chemicals.
  • Oil spills.
  • Plastic waste.

Effects of Water Pollution

  • Death of aquatic organisms.
  • Spread of diseases.
  • Shortage of clean water.
  • Damage to ecosystems.

Prevention of Water Pollution

  • Treat industrial waste before disposal.
  • Proper waste management.
  • Avoid dumping plastics into water.
  • Protect water sources.
