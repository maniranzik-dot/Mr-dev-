Unit 7: Mole Concept

Meaning of Mole

A mole is the amount of substance that contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles. This number is called Avogadro's number.

Important Formulae

Number of moles = Mass ÷ Molar mass

n = m / M

Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance measured in grams per mole (g/mol).

Examples

Find moles in 18g of water (H₂O)

Molar mass of H₂O = 18g/mol
Moles = 18 ÷ 18 = 1 mole

Uses of Mole Concept

  • Calculating amounts of reactants.
  • Balancing chemical equations.
  • Finding masses of products.

Unit 8: Oxides

Meaning of Oxide

An oxide is a compound formed when oxygen combines with another element.

Types of Oxides

  • Basic oxides: Oxides of metals that react with acids.
    Example: MgO
  • Acidic oxides: Oxides of non-metals that react with bases.
    Example: CO₂
  • Amphoteric oxides: React with both acids and bases.
    Example: Al₂O₃
  • Neutral oxides: Do not react with acids or bases.
    Example: CO

Formation of Oxides

Metal + Oxygen → Metal oxide

Example:
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Unit 9: Electrolysis and Non-electrolysis

Meaning of Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the chemical decomposition of an ionic compound using electricity.

Parts of Electrolysis

  • Electrolyte
  • Anode (positive electrode)
  • Cathode (negative electrode)
  • Power source

Example: Electrolysis of Water

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

Non-electrolytes

Non-electrolytes are substances that do not conduct electricity because they do not produce ions.

Applications of Electrolysis

  • Electroplating.
  • Extraction of metals.
  • Purification of metals.

Unit 10: Organic Chemistry

Meaning of Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon compounds, especially hydrocarbons.

Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

Main Groups

  • Alkanes: Saturated hydrocarbons with single bonds.
    Example: Methane (CH₄)
  • Alkenes: Unsaturated hydrocarbons with double bonds.
    Example: Ethene (C₂H₄)
  • Alkynes: Hydrocarbons with triple bonds.
    Example: Ethyne (C₂H₂)

Uses of Organic Compounds

  • Making fuels.
  • Production of plastics.
  • Manufacturing medicines.
