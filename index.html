Unit 7: Simple Machines

1. Meaning of Simple Machine

A simple machine is a device that makes work easier by changing the size or direction of a force.

2. Importance of Simple Machines

  • They reduce the effort needed to do work.
  • They help to lift heavy loads.
  • They make work faster and easier.

3. Types of Simple Machines

  • Lever: A rigid bar that turns around a fixed point called a fulcrum.
  • Pulley: A wheel with a groove used with a rope to lift loads.
  • Wheel and axle: A wheel attached to a smaller rod.
  • Inclined plane: A sloping surface used to raise loads.
  • Screw: An inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder.
  • Wedge: A tool used for cutting or splitting.

4. Mechanical Advantage

Mechanical advantage shows how much a machine multiplies the applied force.

Formula: Mechanical Advantage = Load ÷ Effort

5. Efficiency of a Machine

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output work to input work.

Formula: Efficiency = (Output work ÷ Input work) × 100%

Unit 8: Kinetic Theory and States of Matter

1. Kinetic Theory of Matter

Kinetic theory explains that all matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving.

Main Ideas of Kinetic Theory

  • Matter is made of small particles.
  • Particles are always moving.
  • There are spaces between particles.
  • Particles attract each other.
  • Increasing temperature increases particle movement.

2. States of Matter

(a) Solid State

A solid has a fixed shape and fixed volume.

  • Particles are closely packed.
  • Particles only vibrate in fixed positions.
  • Solids are difficult to compress.
(b) Liquid State

A liquid has a fixed volume but no fixed shape.

  • Particles are close together.
  • Particles can move around.
  • Liquids take the shape of their container.
(c) Gaseous State

A gas has no fixed shape and no fixed volume.

  • Particles are far apart.
  • Particles move freely and quickly.
  • Gases can be compressed easily.

3. Changes of State

  • Melting: Solid changes into liquid.
  • Freezing: Liquid changes into solid.
  • Evaporation: Liquid changes into gas.
  • Condensation: Gas changes into liquid.
  • Sublimation: Solid changes directly into gas.
