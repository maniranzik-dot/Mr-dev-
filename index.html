Unit 7: Electromagnetic Induction

7.1 Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an electromotive force (emf), and hence current in a closed circuit, when the magnetic flux linking the circuit changes.

7.2 Magnetic Flux

Magnetic flux is a measure of the magnetic field passing through a given surface.

Φ = BA cos θ

Where:
Φ = magnetic flux
B = magnetic field strength
A = area
θ = angle between the magnetic field and the normal to the surface

7.3 Faraday's Law

Faraday's law states that the induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.

ε = -N ΔΦ / Δt

The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

7.4 Lenz's Law

Lenz's law states that the direction of an induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.

7.5 Factors Affecting Induced EMF

  • Speed of movement of the magnet or coil
  • Strength of the magnetic field
  • Number of turns in the coil
  • Area of the coil
  • Rate of change of magnetic flux

7.6 Applications

  • Electric generators
  • Transformers
  • Microphones
  • Induction cookers
  • Some electrical measuring devices

Unit 8: Electric Power Transmission

8.1 Electric Power

Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred or converted.

P = VI

Where P is power in watts, V is voltage in volts and I is current in amperes.

8.2 Power Transmission

Electricity generated at power stations is transmitted over long distances to consumers.

8.3 Why High Voltage is Used

For a given power, increasing the transmission voltage reduces the current. Since power loss in transmission lines depends on the square of current, reducing current reduces energy losses.

P_loss = I²R

8.4 Transformers

A transformer is an electrical device used to increase or decrease alternating voltage.

  • Step-up transformer: increases voltage.
  • Step-down transformer: decreases voltage.

8.5 Transformer Relationship

Vs / Vp = Ns / Np

Where:
Vs = secondary voltage
Vp = primary voltage
Ns = number of secondary turns
Np = number of primary turns

8.6 Importance of Efficient Transmission

  • Reduces energy losses.
  • Reduces heating of transmission wires.
  • Allows electricity to travel long distances efficiently.
  • Reduces the amount of energy wasted.

Exercise

Why is electricity transmitted at high voltage?

High voltage allows the same power to be transmitted using a smaller current, reducing energy loss in the transmission lines.

Unit 9: Electric Power Intensive

9.1 Electrical Energy

Electrical energy is the energy transferred by an electric current.

E = Pt

Where E is energy, P is power and t is time.

9.2 Electrical Power

P = VI

Using Ohm's law, electrical power can also be written as:

P = I²R
P = V²/R

9.3 Electrical Energy Consumption

Electrical energy used by appliances depends on their power rating and the time for which they operate.

Energy = Power × Time

9.4 Kilowatt-hour

The kilowatt-hour (kWh) is commonly used as a unit of electrical energy consumption.

1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

9.5 Power Rating of Appliances

The power rating of an appliance indicates the rate at which it uses or transfers electrical energy under specified conditions.

9.6 Reducing Electricity Consumption

  • Switch off unused appliances.
  • Use energy-efficient appliances.
  • Avoid unnecessary lighting.
  • Use appliances efficiently.
  • Maintain electrical equipment properly.

Unit 10: House Electric Installation

10.1 Domestic Electrical Installation

House electrical installation involves connecting electrical appliances, lighting and protective devices safely to an electrical supply.

10.2 Main Components

  • Service cable
  • Energy meter
  • Main switch
  • Distribution board
  • Fuses or circuit breakers
  • Live wire
  • Neutral wire
  • Earth wire

10.3 Live Wire

The live wire carries the alternating potential difference from the supply to the electrical installation.

10.4 Neutral Wire

The neutral wire provides the return path for current in a normal domestic circuit.

10.5 Earth Wire

The earth wire provides a low-resistance path for fault current and helps protect people from electric shock.

10.6 Fuse

A fuse is a safety device containing a wire designed to melt when the current becomes too large, breaking the circuit.

10.7 Circuit Breaker

A circuit breaker automatically opens a circuit when it detects an abnormally high current or another specified fault condition.

10.8 Earthing

Earthing connects exposed conductive parts of electrical equipment to the Earth so that a fault current can flow safely and protective devices can disconnect the supply.

10.9 Electrical Safety

  • Never touch electrical equipment with wet hands.
  • Do not overload sockets.
  • Replace damaged cables.
  • Use correctly rated fuses and circuit breakers.
  • Keep electrical equipment away from water.
  • Switch off the supply before appropriate maintenance.
  • Use qualified electricians for electrical installation work.

10.10 Parallel Connection in Houses

Domestic appliances are generally connected in parallel so that each appliance receives the supply voltage independently and can be switched on or off without stopping the others.
