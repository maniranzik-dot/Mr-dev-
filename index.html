Unit 1: Chemical Bonding

Meaning of Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonding is the force of attraction that holds atoms or ions together to form molecules and compounds.

Why Atoms Form Bonds

  • To become more stable.
  • To obtain a stable outer electron arrangement.
  • To lower their energy.

Types of Chemical Bonds

1. Ionic Bond

An ionic bond is formed through transfer of electrons, usually from a metal to a non-metal.

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl

2. Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share electrons.

H + H → H₂

3. Metallic Bond

Metallic bonding is the attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons.

Bond Formation Example
Ionic Transfer of electrons NaCl
Covalent Sharing of electrons H₂O
Metallic Metal ions and free electrons Cu

Unit 2: Trends in Properties of Elements in the Periodic Table

Periodic Table

The periodic table arranges elements according to atomic number and their repeating chemical properties.

Groups and Periods

  • Groups are vertical columns.
  • Periods are horizontal rows.
Property Across a Period Down a Group
Atomic radius Generally decreases Generally increases
Ionisation energy Generally increases Generally decreases
Metallic character Generally decreases Generally increases

Group 1

Group 1 elements are alkali metals. Examples include lithium, sodium and potassium.

Li → Na → K

Group 17

Group 17 contains halogens such as fluorine, chlorine and bromine.

Unit 5: Categories of Chemical Reactions

Chemical Reaction

A chemical reaction is a process in which reactants are changed into new substances called products.

1. Combination

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

2. Decomposition

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

3. Displacement

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

4. Neutralisation

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Signs of a Reaction

  • Colour change.
  • Gas formation.
  • Precipitate formation.
  • Temperature change.
  • Light production.

Unit 6: Preparation and Identification of Salts

Meaning of Salt

A salt is an ionic compound formed when hydrogen ions in an acid are replaced by metal ions or ammonium ions.

Preparation

Acid + Metal

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

Acid + Base

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Acid + Carbonate

CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

Identification

Ion Test Observation

SO₄²⁻ Barium solution White precipitate
CO₃²⁻ Dilute acid Carbon dioxide gas

Unit 7: Mole Concept

Meaning of Mole

A mole is an amount of substance containing approximately 6.02 × 10²³ particles.

Nₐ = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

Number of Moles

n = m / M

n = number of moles
m = mass in grams
M = molar mass in g/mol

Example:

Find the number of moles in 18 g of H₂O.

Molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol

n = 18 / 18 = 1 mol

Number of Particles

Particles = n × 6.02 × 10²³

Unit 9: Electrolysis and Non-electrolysis

Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic substance using electrical energy.

Main Parts

  • Electrolyte.
  • Anode.
  • Cathode.
  • Power source.
  • Connecting wires.
🔋 Battery

+ → ANODE | ELECTROLYTE | CATHODE ← −

Anions → Anode
Cations → Cathode

Electrolysis of Water

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

Electrolytes

Electrolytes conduct electricity in molten or aqueous form because they contain mobile ions.

Non-electrolytes

Non-electrolytes do not produce mobile ions and therefore do not conduct electricity in solution.

Applications

  • Electroplating.
  • Extraction of metals.
  • Purification of metals.

Unit 10: Organic Chemistry

Meaning

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon compounds.

Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.

Family Main Bond Example
Alkanes Single bond CH₄
Alkenes Double bond C₂H₄
Alkynes Triple bond C₂H₂

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons.

CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Examples: CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈

Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a carbon-carbon double bond.

CₙH₂ₙ

Example: C₂H₄ (ethene)

Alkynes

Alkynes contain a carbon-carbon triple bond.

CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

Example: C₂H₂ (ethyne)

Uses

  • Fuels.
  • Plastics.
  • Medicines.
  • Solvents.
  • Manufacturing materials.
