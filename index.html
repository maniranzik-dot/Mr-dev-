📖 Unit 8: Kinetic Theory and States of Matter

1. Kinetic Theory of Matter

The kinetic theory explains that all matter is made up of tiny particles that are always moving.

2. Main Ideas of Kinetic Theory

  • Matter is made of small particles.
  • Particles are always in motion.
  • Particles have spaces between them.
  • Particles attract each other.
  • The speed of particles increases when temperature increases.

3. States of Matter

Matter exists mainly in three states:

  • Solid
  • Liquid
  • Gas

4. Solids

A solid has a fixed shape and fixed volume.

Properties of solids:

  • Particles are closely packed.
  • Particles vibrate about fixed positions.
  • Strong forces exist between particles.

Examples:

Stone, wood, iron and ice.

5. Liquids

A liquid has a fixed volume but no fixed shape.

Properties of liquids:

  • Particles are close together but can move.
  • They can flow.
  • They take the shape of their container.

Examples:

Water, oil and milk.

6. Gases

A gas has no fixed shape and no fixed volume.

Properties of gases:

  • Particles are far apart.
  • Particles move freely and quickly.
  • Gases can be compressed.

Examples:

Oxygen, carbon dioxide and air.

7. Changes of State

Melting

Change from solid to liquid due to heating.

Freezing

Change from liquid to solid due to cooling.

Evaporation

Change from liquid to gas at the surface.

Boiling

Rapid change from liquid to gas throughout the liquid.

Condensation

Change from gas to liquid due to cooling.

Sublimation

Direct change from solid to gas without becoming liquid.

8. Factors Affecting Evaporation

  • Temperature: Higher temperature increases evaporation.
  • Surface area: Larger surface area increases evaporation.
  • Wind speed: More wind increases evaporation.
  • Humidity: Low humidity increases evaporation.

9. Applications of Kinetic Theory

  • Explaining diffusion.
  • Explaining expansion of materials when heated.
  • Understanding changes of state.

💡 Examples

1. Ice melts when heated because particles gain energy and move faster.

2. Perfume spreads through a room due to diffusion of gas particles.

3. Water vapour changes into liquid water during condensation.
