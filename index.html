Unit 6: Chemical Properties of Acids and Bases

6.1 Preparation of Acids and Bases

  • Acids produce hydrogen ions (H⁺) in aqueous solution.
  • Bases produce hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in aqueous solution.
  • Acids can react with metals, bases and carbonates.
  • Bases can neutralise acids.

6.2 Preparation of Hydrogen and Sodium Metal

  • Hydrogen can be prepared by reacting an active metal with a dilute acid.
  • Example: zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid.
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

6.3 Reaction of Acids and Bases

Acid + Base → Salt + Water

Uses and Dangers

  • Acids are used in batteries, industries and laboratories.
  • Bases are used in cleaning products and industry.
  • Strong acids and bases can cause burns and should be handled carefully.
Exercise:

What is neutralisation?

Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a base to form salt and water.

Unit 7: Concentration of Solutions

7.1 Concept of Concentration

Concentration describes the amount of solute dissolved in a given volume of solution.

Concentration = Amount of solute ÷ Volume of solution

7.2 Preparation of Solutions

  • Measure the required mass of solute.
  • Dissolve it in a small amount of distilled water.
  • Transfer the solution into a volumetric flask.
  • Add water up to the required volume.
  • Mix thoroughly.

7.3 Acids and Titrations

Titration is used to determine the concentration of an unknown solution using a solution of known concentration.

C₁V₁ = C₂V₂
Exercise:

What is a solution?

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of a solute and a solvent.

Unit 8: Electrolysis and Its Applications

8.1 Introduction to Oxidation-Reduction Reactions

  • Oxidation is loss of electrons.
  • Reduction is gain of electrons.
  • Oxidation and reduction occur together in redox reactions.
OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain

8.2 Electrolysis of Solutions

Electrolysis is the chemical decomposition of an ionic compound using electricity.

  • Anode: positive electrode in an electrolytic cell.
  • Cathode: negative electrode in an electrolytic cell.
  • Cations move towards the cathode.
  • Anions move towards the anode.

8.3 Electrolysis of Molten Compounds

Molten ionic compounds contain freely moving ions and can conduct electricity.

8.4 Applications of Electrolysis

  • Electroplating
  • Extraction of metals
  • Purification of metals
  • Manufacture of chemicals
Exercise:

What happens at the cathode?

Reduction occurs at the cathode because positive ions gain electrons.

Unit 9: Structure and Properties of Alkenes and Alcohols

9.1 Nomenclature of Alkenes

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond.

General formula of alkenes: CₙH₂ₙ
  • Ethene: C₂H₄
  • Propene: C₃H₆
  • Butene: C₄H₈

9.2 Sources of Alkenes

Alkenes can be obtained from petroleum and by cracking larger hydrocarbons.

9.3 Properties of Alkenes

  • They undergo addition reactions.
  • They can decolourise bromine water.
  • They are generally flammable.

9.4 Uses of Alkenes

  • Manufacture of plastics
  • Production of alcohols
  • Manufacture of chemicals

9.5 Nomenclature and Structure of Alcohols

Alcohols contain the hydroxyl functional group (–OH).

  • Methanol: CH₃OH
  • Ethanol: C₂H₅OH
  • Propanol: C₃H₇OH

9.6 Preparation of Alcohols

Ethanol can be prepared by fermentation of sugars.

Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide

9.7 Properties of Alcohols

  • Many alcohols are flammable.
  • Lower alcohols are soluble in water.
  • They can be oxidised to other compounds.

9.8 Uses and Misuse of Alcohol

  • Used as solvents.
  • Used as fuels.
  • Used in manufacture of medicines and chemicals.
  • Excessive alcohol consumption can cause serious health and social problems.
Exercise:

What is the functional group of alcohols?

The functional group of alcohols is the hydroxyl group, –OH.

Unit 10: Carboxylic Acids

10.1 General Formula and Nomenclature

Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl functional group –COOH.

General formula: R–COOH
  • Methanoic acid: HCOOH
  • Ethanoic acid: CH₃COOH
  • Propanoic acid: C₂H₅COOH

10.2 Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids can be prepared by oxidation of suitable alcohols.

Ethanol + Oxygen → Ethanoic acid + Water

10.3 Properties of Carboxylic Acids

  • They have acidic properties.
  • They react with bases to form salts and water.
  • They react with carbonates to produce carbon dioxide.
  • Lower carboxylic acids are soluble in water.
Exercise:

What functional group is present in carboxylic acids?

The carboxyl group, –COOH.
