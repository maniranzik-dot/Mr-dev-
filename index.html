Unit 1: Graphs of Linear Motion

1.1 Linear Motion

Linear motion is the motion of an object along a straight line. The motion of an object can be represented using graphs.

1.2 Distance-Time Graph

A distance-time graph shows how the distance travelled by an object changes with time.

  • A horizontal line means the object is at rest.
  • A straight sloping line represents uniform motion.
  • A steeper slope represents greater speed.
  • A curved line represents changing speed.
Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Speed = Gradient of a distance-time graph

1.3 Displacement-Time Graph

A displacement-time graph shows how the displacement of an object changes with time. The gradient of the graph gives velocity.

Velocity = Change in displacement ÷ Change in time

1.4 Velocity-Time Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how the velocity of an object changes with time.

  • Horizontal line → constant velocity.
  • Positive slope → positive acceleration.
  • Negative slope → negative acceleration.
  • Area under the graph → displacement.
Acceleration = Change in velocity ÷ Time

1.5 Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time. The SI unit of acceleration is m/s².

Exercise

A car increases its velocity from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 seconds. Calculate its acceleration.

Acceleration = (Final velocity − Initial velocity) ÷ Time

a = (30 − 10) ÷ 5

a = 4 m/s²

Unit 2: Friction Force and Newton's Laws of Motion

2.1 Friction Force

Friction is a force that opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion between two surfaces in contact.

2.2 Types of Friction

  • Static friction
  • Sliding friction
  • Rolling friction

2.3 Advantages of Friction

  • It allows us to walk.
  • It allows vehicles to move and stop.
  • It allows writing.
  • It helps objects remain in position.

2.4 Disadvantages of Friction

  • Causes wear and tear.
  • Produces unwanted heat.
  • Causes energy loss.
  • Reduces efficiency of machines.

2.5 Newton's First Law

Newton's first law states that an object remains at rest or continues moving with uniform velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by a resultant external force.

Inertia: The tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or motion.

2.6 Newton's Second Law

Newton's second law states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the resultant force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.

F = ma

F = force in newtons (N)
m = mass in kilograms (kg)
a = acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²)

2.7 Newton's Third Law

Newton's third law states that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

2.8 Linear Momentum

p = mv

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity. Its SI unit is kg m/s.

2.9 Impulse

Impulse = Force × Time
I = Ft

Impulse is equal to the change in momentum.

2.10 Conservation of Linear Momentum

In an isolated system, the total linear momentum before an interaction is equal to the total linear momentum after the interaction.

Total momentum before = Total momentum after

Exercise

A body of mass 5 kg accelerates at 4 m/s². Calculate the resultant force.

F = ma

F = 5 × 4

F = 20 N

Unit 3: Applications of Atmospheric Pressure

3.1 Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of the Earth's atmosphere on a surface.

Pressure = Force ÷ Area
P = F/A

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa).

3.2 Why Atmospheric Pressure Exists

Atmospheric pressure exists because air has mass and is attracted towards the Earth by gravity.

3.3 Factors Affecting Atmospheric Pressure

  • Altitude
  • Temperature
  • Air density
  • Humidity

Atmospheric pressure generally decreases as altitude increases.

3.4 Measuring Atmospheric Pressure

A barometer is an instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.

3.5 Mercury Barometer

A mercury barometer uses a column of mercury to measure atmospheric pressure.

P = ρgh

Where:
ρ = density of the liquid
g = gravitational acceleration
h = height of the liquid column

3.6 Applications of Atmospheric Pressure

  • Drinking through a straw
  • Using a syringe
  • Suction pumps
  • Suction cups
  • Barometers
  • Vacuum devices

3.7 Atmospheric Pressure and Weather

Changes in atmospheric pressure are associated with changes in weather. High and low pressure systems can influence wind and weather conditions.

The instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure is a barometer.
