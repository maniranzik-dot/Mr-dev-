🧪 Unit 11: Acids, Bases and pH

1. Meaning of Acids

An acid is a substance that produces hydrogen ions (H⁺) when dissolved in water.

Examples: Hydrochloric acid (HCl), Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄).

2. Properties of Acids

  • They have a sour taste.
  • They turn blue litmus paper red.
  • They react with metals to produce hydrogen gas.
  • They react with bases to form salt and water.

3. Meaning of Bases

A base is a substance that neutralizes an acid and produces hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in water.

Examples: Sodium hydroxide (NaOH), Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂).

4. Properties of Bases

  • They feel slippery.
  • They turn red litmus paper blue.
  • They neutralize acids.

5. Indicators

Indicators are substances used to show whether a solution is acidic or basic.

  • Litmus paper
  • Methyl orange
  • Phenolphthalein
  • Universal indicator

6. pH Scale

The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a solution is.

  • pH less than 7 = Acidic
  • pH equal to 7 = Neutral
  • pH greater than 7 = Basic/Alkaline

7. Neutralization

Neutralization is the reaction between an acid and a base to produce salt and water.

Example:
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

🧂 Unit 12: Inorganic Salts and their Properties

1. Meaning of Salts

A salt is an ionic compound formed when the hydrogen ion of an acid is replaced by a metal or ammonium ion.

Examples:
Sodium chloride (NaCl)
Copper sulphate (CuSO₄)

2. Preparation of Salts

  • Acid + Metal:
    Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen gas

  • Acid + Base:
    Acid + Base → Salt + Water

  • Acid + Carbonate:
    Acid + Carbonate → Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide

3. Properties of Salts

  • Most salts are solids.
  • Some salts dissolve in water.
  • They have high melting points.
  • They may form crystals.

4. Uses of Salts

  • Food seasoning (sodium chloride).
  • Fertilizer production.
  • Medicine production.
  • Industrial processes.

🌬️ Unit 13: Preparation of Oxygen and its Properties

1. Meaning of Oxygen

Oxygen is a colourless, odourless gas that supports respiration and combustion. Its chemical symbol is O₂.

2. Laboratory Preparation of Oxygen

Oxygen can be prepared in the laboratory by decomposition of hydrogen peroxide using manganese dioxide as a catalyst.

Equation:
2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂

3. Collection of Oxygen

  • Collection over water because oxygen is slightly soluble in water.
  • It can also be collected by downward displacement of air.

4. Physical Properties of Oxygen

  • Colourless gas.
  • Odourless.
  • Not poisonous.
  • Slightly soluble in water.

5. Chemical Properties of Oxygen

  • Supports burning.
  • Reacts with metals to form oxides.
  • Reacts with non-metals to form oxides.

6. Uses of Oxygen

  • Breathing by living organisms.
  • Medical oxygen.
  • Welding and cutting metals.
  • Industrial processes.
